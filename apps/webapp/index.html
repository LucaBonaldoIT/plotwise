0.1 · Prologue

Business & data understanding

Business understanding

Gain an in-depth knowledge about the domain and the business problem or opportunity.

  • Involves interviews with the domain experts.
  • Requires the ability to quickly adapt to the context and the domain-specific vocabulary.
  • The most effective approach to carry out interviews is to verify the claims made by domain experts against the data.

Data understanding

Gain an in-depth knowledge about the data:

  • Assessing the available data.
  • Assessing data quality.
  • Carrying out a manual analysis to get initial answers or to form hypotheses about the problem.
  • Verifying the claims made by the domain experts and developing further questions to ask them.
The type of data determines the methods, but data quality determines performance.

Types of data

Different types of data imply different expressiveness and different techniques to be adopted to analyze them. Hover or tap a type.

    0.3

    Structured data

    Structured data are composed of collections of objects described by a set of attributes.

    • Synonyms for object: record, point, case, example, entity, instance, element.
    • An attribute is a property or characteristic of an object. Synonyms: variable, field, characteristic.

    0.4

    Attribute types

    An employee is described by an ID and an age, but it makes no sense to calculate the average employee ID!

    In order to perform meaningful analysis the characteristics of the attributes must be known. The attribute type tells us what properties of the attribute are reflected in the value we use as a measure. A simple way to characterize them is based on the type of operator it makes sense to apply to the values it takes:

    • Diversity =,\ \neq
    • Ordering <,\ \leq,\ >,\ \geq
    • Additivity +,\ -
    • Multiplicativeness *,\ /

    This determines 4 types of attributes: nominal, ordinal, interval and ratio. Each level inherits the operators of the previous one.

    Interval or ratio? Where is zero

    Temperature in Celsius (−10 °C → 0 °C → 10 °C → 20 °C → 30 °C): the difference makes sense (30 °C − 10 °C = 20 °C), but the ratio doesn't: 20 °C is not “the double” of 10 °C, because 0 °C doesn't mean “absence of heat”. How would you describe −10 °C with respect to 20 °C in terms of ratio?

    Weight in kg (0 kg → 10 kg → 20 kg → 30 kg): the difference makes sense (30 kg − 10 kg = 20 kg) and the ratio makes sense too: 20 kg is twice 10 kg, since 0 kg means no weight.

    Alternative classifications

    Binary, discrete and continuous

    • A discrete attribute has a finite number or an infinite countable set of values, normally represented by integers or labels.
    • A continuous attribute takes real values.
    • Nominal and ordinal attributes are typically discrete or binary, while interval and ratio attributes are continuous.

    Countably infinite

    An infinite set is said to be countably infinite if its elements can be enumerated by the natural numbers; equivalently, there exists a bijective mapping between the set and \mathbb{N}. Examples:

    • Positive integers, integers
    • Even / odd numbers
    • The Cartesian product of two countably infinite sets
    • Rational numbers
    • All finite strings over a finite alphabet

    0.7

    Asymmetric attributes

    For asymmetric attributes only the presence of a value (non-zero) is relevant; sharing a 0 (a common absence) does not make two objects similar.

    • Study plan: a binary vector with one attribute for each exam offered by the University (1 = chosen, 0 = not chosen).
    • The University offers m = 1{,}000 exams while each student chooses about 20 of them: more than 98% of the values are 0. Compared on all attribute values, every student would look very similar.
    • The similarity between study plans must be based on the common exams only. f_{ab} = number of exams with value a in the first plan and b in the second one.
    Simple Matching Coefficient
    SMC = \frac{f_{11} + f_{00}}{f_{11} + f_{10} + f_{01} + f_{00}}

    Counts both 1–1 and 0–0 matches.

    Jaccard coefficient
    J = \frac{f_{11}}{f_{11} + f_{10} + f_{01}}

    Ignores 0–0 matches.

    Beyond the plain table

    Documents & texts

    Documents are the objects of the analysis; they are described by a vector of terms. Each term is an attribute of the document, and its value is the number of times the term appears in the document.

    Transactions

    A record where:

    • each record (transaction) involves multiple items;
    • items come from a finite set;
    • for example, in a supermarket, the set of products purchased by a customer during a visit constitutes a transaction, while the set of items is the set of all the products sold by the supermarket;
    • the number of items may vary from transaction to transaction.

    Ordered data and time series

    Both involve an order, but there's a fundamental difference related to the meaning of the order and time.

    Ordered data

    Data organized according to a specific order, but the order does not necessarily represent time. You can compare positions or rank data, but there is no implied time interval.

    • Student rankings by score (1st, 2nd, 3rd)
    • Clothing sizes (S, M, L, XL)
    • Satisfaction levels (1–5 stars)

    Time series data

    Data collected in temporal sequence, where each value has a precise timestamp, often with regular or variable frequency. Time is intrinsic; patterns, trends and correlations depend on the temporal sequence.

    • Daily temperature
    • Stock prices minute by minute
    • Hourly energy consumption
    Ordinal data are the standard in…
    1. Market research and user experience. In surveys like “rank your preferences”, users are asked to order products, services or features from most to least important.
    2. Psychology and cognitive sciences. Preference tests or cognitive games in which participants must rank stimuli (images, words, scenarios) according to liking, emotional response or perceived difficulty. The ranking becomes research data to understand perceptions and mental patterns.
    3. Medicine and health. In some clinical studies, patients rank symptoms or side effects by severity or impact on quality of life. The ranking helps researchers assess subjective priorities without requiring precise numerical scales.
    4. Recommendation systems. In Netflix, Spotify or Amazon, users can rank or prioritize movies, songs or products they prefer. The algorithm uses this information to personalize suggestions, even without explicit numerical ratings.

    Ordering becomes an indirect method of data collection, because it allows subjective perceptions, preferences or relative priorities to be transformed into structured information without requiring absolute numbers.

      The Titanic dataset

      The sinking of the Titanic is one of the most infamous shipwrecks in history. On April 15, 1912, during her maiden voyage, the widely considered “unsinkable” RMS Titanic sank after colliding with an iceberg. Unfortunately, there weren't enough lifeboats for everyone on board, resulting in the death of 1502 out of 2224 passengers and crew. While there was some element of luck involved in surviving, it seems some groups of people were more likely to survive than others. The Kaggle training set has 891 passengers.
